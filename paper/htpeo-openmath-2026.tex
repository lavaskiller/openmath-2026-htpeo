\begin{abstract}
We report what team HTPeo obtained during the OpenMath 2026 event (27 September -- 3 October 2026), in which only
formalized results count. (1)~A weighted two-colouring template on 1024 blocks shows
$c_4 \le 200137750014677779172692777477/6640289795261907019148161833841 \approx 0.0301399120$
for the $K_4$ Ramsey multiplicity constant, below the previously best value $10486266368/768^4\approx0.0301422734$
(McKay); the whole chain from the template to the bound on $c_4$ is checked in Lean~4 with standard axioms only.
(2)~For the Dvo\v{r}\'ak--Mohar--\v{S}\'amal conjecture (star chromatic index of subcubic multigraphs at most~6) we do
\emph{not} prove the conjecture; we prove in Lean star $5$-edge-colourings of several infinite families, star
$6$-edge-colourability of all bridgeless cubic loopless multigraphs on at most 14 vertices, an equivalent form of the
conjecture, and a reduction to named open hypotheses. (3)~We formalize known results attached to 16 Erd\H{o}s
problems, stated exactly as in the formal-conjectures repository, and Sch\"onberger's and Petersen's theorems for
Mathlib's \code{SimpleGraph} (17 families, 24 theorems). We also list our results on the competition's scored tasks
(``hills''), three of which carry Lean certificates, and we state plainly what is not checked, what was already known,
and what the work cost. Most of the mathematics and all of the Lean code were produced by AI systems under human
direction, and no human review of the proofs was done (Section~\ref{sec:provenance}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
OpenMath 2026 (the handbook is titled \emph{Open Problems Hack at MIT}) ran from a status freeze at noon Eastern on
27 September 2026 to the submission deadline at 00:00 EDT on 3 October. Problems are posed as \emph{hills} on the
AutoLab platform: versioned tasks with an evaluator and a leaderboard. In the handbook's words a passing hill ``is not
itself a mathematical proof''; formal checking, statement fidelity, literature status, attribution and review are
separate gates. Our Ramsey and DMS entries are proposed under mode M3A (open problems outside the curated focus set),
the formalizations under M2 (known mathematics, counted in families).

All Lean results below use Lean~4.33.1 and Mathlib v4.33.1 (the Erd\H{o}s files: Mathlib as pinned by
\code{google-deepmind/formal-conjectures} at commit \code{df3f12d7}). Every claimed theorem compiles with exit code~0
and \code{\#print axioms} lists only \code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice} and \code{Quot.sound}. No claimed
theorem depends on \code{sorry}, an added axiom, or \code{native\_decide}. The repository contains the sources, the
build logs with the axiom output, checksums, and the exact commands.

\section{The $K_4$ Ramsey multiplicity constant}
Let $\mathrm{minMono}_{K_4}(m)$ be the least number of monochromatic $K_4$ over all red/blue colourings of the edges
of $K_m$. The constant $c_4=\lim_m \mathrm{minMono}_{K_4}(m)/\binom m4$ exists. Thomason disproved Erd\H{o}s'
conjecture $c_4=1/32$; the best published upper bound we are aware of is McKay's
$10486266368/768^4\approx0.0301422734$, announced in the closing note of Parczyk, Pokutta, Spiegel and Szab\'o
(arXiv:2206.04036v3), obtained by local search from a 768-vertex Cayley graph; the best lower bound cited there is
$0.0296$ (Grzesik, Lee, Lidick\'y, Volec). McKay's value is the reference of the AutoLab hill
\code{clique-cluster-ramsey-multiplicity}.

\paragraph{Result.} A symmetric weighted template on 1024 blocks (integer weights, total $Q=50762939$, each pair of
blocks and each block's interior coloured red or blue) has monochromatic-$K_4$ density
\[
\frac{N}{Q^4}=\frac{200137750014677779172692777477}{6640289795261907019148161833841}\approx 0.030139911990,
\]
i.e.\ hill metric $\lceil 10^{12}\cdot N/Q^4\rceil = 30{,}139{,}911{,}990$ against the reference $30{,}142{,}273{,}432$.
Blowing the template up gives colourings of $K_m$ for all large $m$ with at most that density, hence
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem ramseyMultK4_limit_lt_ref :
    ∃ L : ℝ, Filter.Tendsto (fun m : ℕ => (minMonoK4 m : ℝ) / (m.choose 4 : ℝ)) Filter.atTop (nhds L) ∧
      L < 10486266368 / 768 ^ 4
\end{lstlisting}
together with \code{ramseyMultK4\_le\_sol} (the limit is at most the fraction above), \code{sol\_density},
\code{sol\_ppt}, \code{sol\_symm} and \code{minMonoK4\_density\_le\_sol} (every $m\ge4$ satisfies the bound).

\paragraph{How the proof is checked.} The finite computation (the number of monochromatic $K_4$ in the weighted
template) is split into $2\times1024$ per-block kernel evaluations (\code{decide +kernel}) on a packed bit-mask
representation whose agreement with the literal specification is proved once for all templates; the blow-up lemma,
the existence of the limit (vertex-deletion averaging) and the comparison with the reference are ordinary Lean proofs.
The certificate has 2048 generated chunk modules; it built with two processes in about two hours (3.9 CPU hours, at
most 3.3~GB per process). The transcription of the template from JSON to Lean is done by a script and checked by a
second script; it is not itself proved.

\paragraph{How the template was found.} Starting from the hill's 768-vertex Cayley seed: simulated annealing and tabu
search on the colour orbits under the automorphism group, splitting of near-twin blocks up to 1024 blocks, continuous
weight optimisation with rounding to integers, and basin hopping; every candidate was re-scored exactly with the
hill's own evaluator. A first template (density\_ppt 30,139,933,996, 2 October) was improved on 3 October and its
certificate regenerated. On the hill's validation board the result is first of 13 accounts at the time of writing,
224,043~ppt ahead of the second. It has no final-mode (held-out) evaluation; one attempt failed for a technical reason.
The improvement is numerical (about $2.4\times10^{-6}$ in $c_4$) and does not determine $c_4$.

\section{The Dvo\v{r}\'ak--Mohar--\v{S}\'amal conjecture}
The conjecture states that every subcubic multigraph has star chromatic index at most~6 (a star edge-colouring is
proper and has no bichromatic path or cycle of length four); the best published general bound we know is~7.
\textbf{We do not prove the conjecture.} Proved in Lean (\code{entries/dms-star6}):
\begin{itemize}
\item star $5$-edge-colourings of the flower snarks $J_n$ ($n$ odd, $n\ge5$), the Goldberg snarks, the generalized
Petersen graphs $GP(n,k)$ for all $1\le k\le 15$ and $n\ge 2k+1$ (except $GP(3,1)$), the M\"obius ladders and
Petersen-type inflations, each as a statement for the whole infinite family;
\item star $6$-edge-colourability of every bridgeless cubic loopless multigraph on at most 14 vertices;
\item an equivalent form \code{dms\_iff\_cubic16} reducing the conjecture to bridgeless cubic multigraphs on at least
16 vertices together with a condition on their leaf sets;
\item a chain ``named open hypotheses $\Rightarrow$ conjecture''; each hypothesis is open.
\end{itemize}
The graph families are given as explicit edge lists; their agreement with the textbook definitions is checked by a
script, not proved as an isomorphism. These results came out of an automated multi-agent ``harness'' that kept a graph
of verified facts and cross-checked them with a second model family; the informal studies of the remaining core case
(cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graphs) are in the archive and are not Lean-checked.

\paragraph{Kobon triangles, 39 lines.} Starting from the F\"uredi--Pal\'asti arrangement (468 triangles), simulated
annealing on the line parameters found 39 lines with integer coefficients bounding 471 triangles. The Lean certificate
(\code{Kobon.kobon\_nonoverlapping}) states that there are 39 pairwise distinct lines and 471 index triples, each
bounding a non-degenerate triangle whose open interior meets none of the lines, the triangles being pairwise disjoint;
\code{Kobon.certificate} checks the list against the signed hill report. The upper bound is
$\lfloor 39\cdot37/3\rfloor=481$. Whether 471 exceeds every published value for 39 lines was checked only by an AI
helper (OEIS A006066 and one public gallery), \textbf{not by a human}; we do not claim it as new.

\paragraph{Busy Beaver 6.} The Lean project (core Lean, no Mathlib) proves that the machine above halts after exactly
249,881 steps, reaches all six states, and leaves 554 ones on a span of 735 cells, and that it is accepted by the hill
exactly when the budget is at least 249,881. It reproduces the board's best value. That 249,881 is the largest halting
time below the hidden budget is \emph{not} proved; a separate computation excluded 31 of 32 structural classes of
machines and was stopped before the last class was finished.

\paragraph{Grothendieck constant.} For the CHSH matrix with rational unit vectors the certificate proves that every
constant $K$ satisfying the finite-dimensional Grothendieck inequality has $K\ge1393/985$, and with exact vectors
$K\ge\sqrt2$; Tsirelson's bound for this matrix shows that no witness on it can score higher. All of this is classical.

\section{Erd\H{o}s problem \#1038}\label{sec:1038}
Two team members produced a proof (with Claude, cross-checked with GPT) that the infimum of the measure of
$\{x:|f(x)|<1\}$ over monic real polynomials with all roots in $[-1,1]$ equals an explicit constant
$D\approx1.8344304757$, and that the supremum is $2\sqrt2$, and formalized it in Lean~4.34.1 (315 modules). During
the work they found that a proposed solution with the same approach had been published and that a Lean proof was
already public (S.~Wang; imported into plby/lean-proofs on 15 September 2026). The team's development is a
reconstruction with a different implementation. \textbf{We do not claim it.} The package and our partial rebuild with
Lean~4.33.1 (supremum and classical lower bound re-checked; the exact infimum not, for memory reasons) are in
\code{entries/erdos-1038}.

\section{Provenance, review and cost}\label{sec:provenance}
The DMS results came from an automated harness on a 16-core server: Claude agents proposed and proved lemmas, a Lean
verification service accepted facts, and GPT models audited them; about 786 informal facts were recorded. The Ramsey
search, the certificates, the Erd\H{o}s formalizations and the hill work were done in Claude Code and Codex sessions
directed by team members. Recorded AI usage of the team lead's server and laptop was about $8.1\times10^7$ output
tokens in 2,145 sessions, all under subscription plans; the other members' usage was not recorded as numbers.
Human review: none. Woohyuk Kang and Hyunjin Lee declared that they did no human review, and the other members did not state one. All proofs were written by AI models and checked by the Lean kernel and by scripts.

\section*{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item The Ramsey bound improves the constant numerically only; $c_4$ is not determined.
\item The DMS conjecture is not proved; every hypothesis of our reduction is open.
\item The M2 results are known mathematics; ``first formalization'' rests on searches with stated limits.
\item The hill evaluators themselves are not formalized; where we compare with them, we compare against a hand
transcription or against the signed report.
\item Literature statements marked as unchecked by a human have not been checked by a human.
\end{itemize}
