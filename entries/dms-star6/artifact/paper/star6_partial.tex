\begin{abstract}
Dvo\v{r}\'ak, Mohar and \v{S}\'amal conjectured that every subcubic multigraph has star chromatic index at most~$6$; the best published general bound is~$7$. We do \emph{not} prove the conjecture. We report a chain of reductions, obtained with the help of an automated proof-search system and checked by its verification pipeline, which shows that the conjecture (for finite loopless multigraphs) follows from five explicit statements about $2$-cut-reduced cubic multigraphs: two ``far-exchange'' statements on digon insertions of cyclically $4$-edge-connected simple cubic graphs, a pole-dominance statement, a restricted perfect-matching colouring property at triangles carrying one digon, and a leaf-attachment statement. The assembly of this chain, together with all finite small-case facts except three census-level hypotheses, is formalized in \Lean{} with only the standard axioms. We also record Lean-certified finite base cases (all $2$-cut-reduced cubic multigraphs on $10$ and $12$ vertices, and the digon insertions of small cyclically $4$-edge-connected hosts), partial results on each open statement, star colourings of several infinite families (generalized Petersen graphs $GP(k,2)$ with the spokes as a colour class, flower and Goldberg snarks, covers of the Petersen graph and of $K_4$), and an explicit account of which parts are machine-checked and which rest on model-based verification only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A \emph{star edge colouring} of a multigraph is a proper edge colouring with no bicoloured path or cycle of length four. The \emph{star chromatic index} $\chis(G)$ is the least number of colours in such a colouring. Dvo\v{r}\'ak, Mohar and \v{S}\'amal~\cite{DMS13} proved that every subcubic multigraph satisfies $\chis(G)\le 7$ and conjectured the following.

\begin{conjecture}[\hyp{DMS}, \cite{DMS13}]
Every subcubic multigraph has star chromatic index at most $6$.
\end{conjecture}

The bound $6$ would be sharp ($K_{3,3}$ needs six colours~\cite{DMS13}). Partial results include the bound $6$ for subcubic multigraphs of maximum average degree below $5/2$ and the bound $5$ below $24/11$ (Lei, Shi and Song~\cite{LSS18}; see also~\cite{LSSW18}), and bounds for special classes such as Halin graphs and generalized Petersen graphs~\cite{HLW20,Zhu21,OD24}. The conjecture remains open.

\subsection*{What this paper contains}
This is a \emph{partial-results} report. Its mathematical content was produced by an automated multi-agent proof-search system (a fork of the \textsf{Danus} harness) working on the conjecture for \emph{finite loopless} multigraphs, and every result stated here is a fact that passed the system's verification pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:status} explains exactly what that means, result by result). Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{A reduction theorem} (Main Theorem~\ref{thm:main}): DMS for loopless multigraphs follows from five explicitly stated statements \hyp{FE-EXT-D}$_{10}$, \hyp{FE-EXIST-D}$_{10}$, \hyp{POLE}, \hyp{TD-TRI}, \hyp{II$_D$} about $2$-cut-reduced cubic multigraphs on at least $10$ (in one case $6$) vertices. Along the way we record several equivalent or sufficient single-statement forms (Theorems~\ref{thm:I-II}--\ref{thm:hole}).
\item \textbf{A formal version} (Theorem~\ref{thm:lean-cs4}): the whole reduction, including all its finite inputs except three census-level finite hypotheses, is proved in \Lean{} with standard axioms only.
\item \textbf{Finite certified cases} (Section~\ref{sec:finite}): certificates for all small cases used, most of them kernel-checked in \Lean.
\item \textbf{Partial progress on each open statement} (Section~\ref{sec:partial}), e.g.\ \hyp{POLE} holds up to $16$ vertices and \hyp{II$_D$} up to $14$ vertices.
\item \textbf{Class results} (Section~\ref{sec:classes}), with a comparison to the literature.
\item \textbf{Open problems and obstacles} (Section~\ref{sec:open}) and an honest \textbf{verification status} (Section~\ref{sec:status}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Caveats up front}
None of the five hypotheses of Main Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is proved; each is supported by computation only in the ranges stated in Sections~\ref{sec:partial} and~\ref{sec:evidence}. Most of the facts were checked by a language-model verifier and a second-model cross-check audit rather than by a proof assistant; several related facts carry open audit disputes (Section~\ref{sec:status}). The novelty of most results has not been checked against the literature (Section~\ref{sec:novelty}).

\section{Definitions and conventions}\label{sec:prelim}

All multigraphs are finite and loopless; parallel edges are allowed and are distinct edges. Two distinct edges \emph{meet} if they share an endpoint (parallel edges meet). A multigraph is \emph{cubic} (resp.\ \emph{subcubic}) if every vertex is incident with exactly (resp.\ at most) three edges. For $S\subseteq V(X)$, $\delta(S)$ is the set of edges with exactly one endpoint in $S$; $S$ is a \emph{$k$-edge-cut side} if $\emptyset\ne S\ne V(X)$ and $|\delta(S)|=k$. A \emph{perfect matching} (PM) is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint edges covering every vertex. For an edge $g$ and an edge set $N$ we write $[g\in N]\in\{0,1\}$.

\begin{definition}[Star colourings]\label{def:star}
A \emph{star edge colouring with $k$ colours} of $X$ is a map $c\colon E(X)\to\{1,\dots,k\}$ such that (i) distinct edges with a common endpoint get different colours, and (ii) $X$ has no path with exactly four edges (on five distinct vertices) and no cycle with exactly four edges (on four distinct vertices) whose consecutive edges $e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4$ satisfy $c(e_1)=c(e_3)$ and $c(e_2)=c(e_4)$. An edge set $N$ is a \emph{colour class} of $c$ if $N=c^{-1}(\mu)$ for some $\mu$. An \emph{MC colouring} is a star edge colouring with $6$ colours whose colour class $6$ is a perfect matching; $(X,N)$ is \emph{MC-colourable} if $X$ has an MC colouring with $c^{-1}(6)=N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Classes]\label{def:classes}
$\cG$ is the class of connected bridgeless loopless cubic multigraphs. A member $X$ of $\cG$ is \emph{$2$-cut-reduced} if every $2$-edge-cut side $S$ satisfies $|S|=2$ or $|V(X)\setminus S|=2$; $\cD\subseteq\cG$ is the class of $2$-cut-reduced members. $\cS$ is the class of simple $3$-edge-connected cubic graphs. A \emph{cyclic $3$-edge-cut side} of a cubic $X$ is a $3$-edge-cut side $S$ such that both $X[S]$ and $X[V(X)\setminus S]$ contain a cycle; a cubic graph is \emph{cyclically $4$-edge-connected} (c4c) if it is connected and, for every set $R$ of at most three edges, at most one component of $X-R$ contains a cycle. For $H\in\cS$, $s(H)$ is the least size of a cyclic $3$-edge-cut side of~$H$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Digon insertion]\label{def:digon}
For a cubic graph $H$ and $D\subseteq E(H)$, the \emph{digon insertion} $H^D$ replaces every edge $ab\in D$ by two new vertices $u_{ab},v_{ab}$, the edges $au_{ab}$, $v_{ab}b$, and two parallel edges $u_{ab}v_{ab}$. Thus $|V(H^D)|=|V(H)|+2|D|$.
\end{definition}
% fdd83999 (H-ASM): every 2-cut-reduced member of G on >=10 vertices arises as H^D with H in S (Lean: layers 16-18, 81c52cea / c3375be1 / 345a55d7).

\begin{definition}[Leaf graph]\label{def:leaf}
For a loopless cubic multigraph $G_0$ and an edge $g$ of $G_0$ with ends $s\neq t$, the \emph{leaf graph} $T(G_0,g)$ is obtained from $G_0$ by deleting $g$ and adding two new vertices $x,\ell$ and the three edges $sx$, $xt$, $x\ell$. An edge $g$ of $X$ is \emph{eligible} if it has distinct ends $s,t$, no other edge joins $s$ and $t$, and $g$ lies in no $2$-edge-cut of $X$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[EX1-good]\label{def:ex1}
A connected loopless cubic multigraph $X$ is \emph{EX1-good} if for every edge $g$ and every $t\in\{0,1\}$ such that some PM $N$ of $X$ has $[g\in N]=t$, there is a PM $N$ of $X$ with $[g\in N]=t$ such that $(X,N)$ is MC-colourable.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Far-exchange sets]\label{def:fe}
Let $X$ be a loopless cubic multigraph and $P$ a PM of $X$. The \emph{distance} between two edges is the least number of edges of a path from an endpoint of one to an endpoint of the other. A set $C\subseteq E(X)\setminus P$ is a \emph{far-exchange set for $P$} if
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item every edge of $P$ meets at most one edge of $C$;
\item[(b$'$)] no edge of $E(X)\setminus(P\cup C)$ that is parallel to no edge of $C$ has both endpoints incident with edges of $C$;
\item[(c)] any two distinct edges of $P$ that meet no edge of $C$ are at distance at least $3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
% far-exchange sets: 898eb5ab (H-RED6), multigraph form; simple form = contract P09-FE-EXT.

\begin{definition}[Poles and dominance]\label{def:pole}
Let $X$ be a loopless cubic multigraph and $v$ a vertex whose three edges join it to three distinct vertices $x_1,x_2,x_3$. The \emph{pole} $Q(X,v)$ is $X-v$ together with three new vertices $o_t$ and \emph{port edges} $p_t=x_to_t$ ($t=1,2,3$). For $d\colon E(Q)\to\{1,\dots,6\}$, $\Col_t(d)$ is the set of colours of the two edges $f\neq p_t$ at $x_t$, and $\Blk_t(d)$ is the set of those colours $d(f)$, $f=x_tr$, for which $r$ is incident with an edge $f'\ne f$ with $d(f')=d(p_t)$. The map $d$ is an \emph{MC pole colouring} if it is a star edge colouring with $6$ colours and every vertex of $X-v$ is incident with exactly one edge of colour $6$.

Let $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, $\{j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}$. An \emph{admissible outside datum at M-port $i$} consists of pairwise distinct colours $a_1,a_2,a_3$ with $a_i=6$ and sets $B_t\subseteq O_t\subseteq\{1,\dots,6\}$ with $|O_t|=2$ such that: $O_i\subseteq\{1,\dots,5\}\setminus\{a_j,a_k\}$ and $B_i=O_i$; for $t\in\{j,k\}$, $6\in O_t$, $a_t\notin O_t$ and $B_t\subseteq O_t\setminus\{6,a_j,a_k\}$; and not both $a_k\in O_j$ and $a_j\in O_k$. An MC pole colouring $d$ is \emph{compatible} with the datum if $d(p_t)=a_t$ and $\Col_t(d)\cap O_t\cap(\Blk_t(d)\cup B_t)=\emptyset$ for $t=1,2,3$. The pole is \emph{dominant} if
\begin{enumerate}[label=(D\arabic*)]
\item for every $i$ and every admissible outside datum at M-port $i$ there is a compatible MC pole colouring; and
\item for every edge $g$ of $X-v$ and $s\in\{0,1\}$ there is $i$ such that for every admissible outside datum at M-port $i$ there is a compatible MC pole colouring $d$ with $[d(g)=6]=s$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
% poles & dominance: 87fba731 (3-cut brick; novelty "new").

\begin{definition}[The property (TD)]\label{def:td}
Let $X\in\cG$ and let $y_1,y_2,y_3,u,v$ be distinct vertices such that the edges incident with them are exactly $y_1y_2$, $y_1y_3$, $y_2u$, two parallel edges $uv$, $vy_3$, and three edges $e_t=y_tw_t$ with $w_1,w_2,w_3$ distinct and outside $\{y_1,y_2,y_3,u,v\}$ (a triangle carrying one digon); let $U=V(X)\setminus\{y_1,y_2,y_3,u,v\}$. \hyp{TD} holds for $(X;y_1,y_2,y_3,u,v)$ if for every edge $g$ of $X[U]$ and $t\in\{0,1\}$ such that some PM $N\ni e_1,y_2u,vy_3$ has $[g\in N]=t$, there is an MC colouring $c$ of $X$ with $c(e_1)=c(y_2u)=c(vy_3)=6$ and $[c(g)=6]=t$.
\end{definition}

\section{The reduction chain}\label{sec:reduction}

\subsection{From all subcubic multigraphs to two cubic statements}
Consider
\begin{itemize}
\item[\hyp{I}] every bridgeless loopless cubic multigraph has a star edge colouring with $6$ colours;
\item[\hyp{II}] for every bridgeless loopless cubic multigraph $G_0$ and every edge $g$ of $G_0$, $T(G_0,g)$ has a star edge colouring with $6$ colours.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:I-II}
\hyp{I} $\wedge$ \hyp{II} holds if and only if every finite loopless multigraph of maximum degree at most $3$ has a star edge colouring with $6$ colours.
\end{theorem}
% a13aecc6 (llm+crosscheck-audit, passed). Lean: 974f7197 DmsIILean.dms_of_I_II (lean-gate, audit passed).
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
The converse is trivial. For the forward direction one argues by induction on $|E(H)|$. Components are coloured separately. A bridge with edges on both sides is handled by a gluing lemma for star $6$-colourings across a bridge.
% 54d1051a
Otherwise every bridge is pendant; the core $C$ obtained by deleting the leaves is connected, bridgeless, with degrees $2$ and $3$. If $C$ has no degree-$2$ vertex, $H=C$ and \hyp{I} applies. If it has $k\ge 2$ of them, $H$ embeds into a bridgeless cubic multigraph obtained by joining the (existing or new) leaves in a cycle (or a digon if $k=2$), and \hyp{I} applies to it. If $k=1$, suppressing the degree-$2$ vertex $y$ of $C$ yields a bridgeless cubic $G_0$ with a new edge $g$, and $H$ is a sub-multigraph of $T(G_0,g)$, so \hyp{II} applies. Star colourings restrict to sub-multigraphs.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Single-statement sufficient conditions}
Two strengthenings give DMS on their own; both implications are formalized.

\begin{theorem}[CubicSharp5$_s$ implies DMS]\label{thm:cs5}
Suppose every connected bridgeless simple cubic graph other than $K_{3,3}$ and the prism $K_3\square K_2$ has a star edge colouring with $5$ colours. Then \hyp{I} and \hyp{II} hold, and hence every finite loopless multigraph with maximum degree at most $3$ has a star edge colouring with $6$ colours.
\end{theorem}
% b22aa112 (prose, llm+crosscheck-audit, passed; novelty "new"); Lean: d1ef48fd DmsIIsLean.cs5s_dms (Lean 4.20 core, no Mathlib, standard axioms; also builds under 4.33.1), with a41e8f0a (CS5s => DMS_I).
The proof replaces each digon of a cubic multigraph by a rigid $5$-colourable gadget (the prism minus an edge), colours the resulting simple graph with $5$ colours, and uses the sixth colour on one edge of each digon; the leaf case is a finite case analysis on the position of $g$ relative to the digons, which is itself formalized.
% 85bb5f97 (Pi-substitution), 58c4b649 (LeafRecipeLean.step4), 4da62a86 (LeafAllLean.leaf_all), 9c01b9b4 (claim W), 3a172a44, d9fbe390, 1a8aa1c2, 0c35d380 (Pi rigidity, Lean).

\begin{theorem}[HOLE implies DMS]\label{thm:hole}
Suppose that for every $X\in\cG$ not isomorphic to $K_{3,3}$ and every vertex $t$ there are a star edge colouring $c$ of $X$ with $6$ colours and a colour $\beta$ missing from all edges incident with the closed neighbourhood $N[t]$. Then every finite loopless multigraph with maximum degree at most $3$ has a star edge colouring with $6$ colours.
\end{theorem}
% 5c1eb3f5 MGraph.dms_of_hole (Lean 4.20 core, standard axioms; lean-gate + audit passed). Prose part (a) of 336b55e3.

\begin{remark}
The hypotheses of Theorems~\ref{thm:cs5} and~\ref{thm:hole} are open. For CubicSharp$5_s$ the only evidence is computational (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}); we did not pursue these routes further.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Perfect matchings as a colour class}
The main route replaces \hyp{I} by a statement asking for a perfect matching as a colour class.

\begin{itemize}
\item[\hyp{H}] every $X\in\cD$ with $|V(X)|\ge 10$ is EX1-good.
\item[\hyp{II$_D$}] for every $G_0\in\cD$ with $|V(G_0)|\ge 6$ and every eligible edge $g$ of $G_0$, $T(G_0,g)$ has a star edge colouring with $6$ colours.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[EX1 reduction]\label{thm:ex1red}
If \hyp{H} holds, then every $X\in\cG$ with $|V(X)|\ge 10$ is EX1-good; in particular it has a PM $M$ and a star $6$-edge-colouring with $M$ as a colour class.
\end{theorem}
% 0e90cbc9 (EX1-RED; llm+crosscheck-audit passed); Lean: RH2F layers 1-9 (7d291f6c, c64b6bd6, 657e581f, 341b6e5e, 643ccea3, 09796da8, 6e44d3c2, ebeb33c5, 066dc781, 3e79907c).

\begin{theorem}[Reduction to \hyp{H} and the leaf case]\label{thm:rh2}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \hyp{H} $\wedge$ \hyp{II} implies DMS for loopless multigraphs.
\item \hyp{H} $\wedge$ \hyp{II$_D$} implies \hyp{II}; hence \hyp{H} $\wedge$ \hyp{II$_D$} implies DMS for loopless multigraphs.
\end{enumerate}
Both statements are proved in \Lean{} (Lean 4.20 with Mathlib v4.20.0, standard axioms only), and the Lean propositions for \hyp{H}, \hyp{II}, \hyp{II$_D$} and DMS are proved equivalent, in \Lean, to plain restatements of the prose over arbitrary finite types.
\end{theorem}

(a) Components with at most $8$ vertices are handled by a finite base case

\subsection{From \hyp{H} to statements about c4c hosts}
Every member of $\cD$ on at least $10$ vertices is a digon insertion $H^D$ of some $H\in\cS$.
% fdd83999 (H-ASM)
The hypothesis \hyp{H} is split along this structure: the c4c hosts are handled by two ``far-exchange'' statements, cyclic $3$-edge-cuts by a gluing (``brick'') lemma that needs pole dominance of one side, and the one configuration that the brick does not cover---a triangle side of minimum size carrying exactly one digon---by the property \hyp{TD}.

\begin{hypothesis}[The five open statements]\label{stmt:five}
\leavevmode
\begin{itemize}
\item[\hyp{FE-EXT-D}$_{10}$] For every c4c simple cubic graph $H$ with $|V(H)|\ge 10$, every $D\subseteq E(H)$ with $|V(H)|+2|D|\ge 16$, every PM $P$ of $X:=H^D$ and every far-exchange set $C$ for $P$ in $X$, $X$ has a star edge colouring $c$ with $6$ colours such that $c^{-1}(6)=P$ and $c^{-1}(5)=C$.
\item[\hyp{FE-EXIST-D}$_{10}$] For every c4c simple cubic graph $H$ with $|V(H)|\ge 10$, every $D\subseteq E(H)$ with $|V(H)|+2|D|\ge 16$, every edge $g$ of $X:=H^D$ and every $t\in\{0,1\}$, there are a PM $P$ of $X$ with $[g\in P]=t$ and a far-exchange set $C$ for $P$ in~$X$.
\item[\hyp{POLE}] For every $X\in\cD$ with $|V(X)|\ge 10$ and every vertex $v$ of $X$ whose three edges join it to three distinct vertices, the pole $Q(X,v)$ is dominant.
\item[\hyp{TD-TRI}] For every $H\in\cS$ that is not c4c, every cyclic $3$-edge-cut side $S=\{y_1,y_2,y_3\}$ with $|S|=3=s(H)$, and every $D\subseteq E(H)$ with $|V(H)|+2|D|\ge 16$ such that the edges of $D$ incident with $S$ consist of exactly one edge $y_2y_3$ (after renaming), \hyp{TD} holds for $(H^D;y_1,y_2,y_3,u,v)$, where $u,v$ are the new vertices of the digon inserted into $y_2y_3$ ($u$ adjacent to $y_2$).
\item[\hyp{II$_D$}] As above: for every $G_0\in\cD$ with $|V(G_0)|\ge 6$ and every eligible edge $g$, $T(G_0,g)$ is star $6$-edge-colourable.
\end{itemize}
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[Intermediate reductions]\label{thm:hred}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \hyp{FE-EXT-D}$_{10}$ $\wedge$ \hyp{FE-EXIST-D}$_{10}$ implies: for every c4c simple cubic $H$ with $|V(H)|\ge 10$ and every $D$ with $|V(H)|+2|D|\ge 16$, $H^D$ is EX1-good.
\item For c4c simple cubic hosts on at most $8$ vertices the same conclusion holds unconditionally \textup{(Lemma SMALLHOST-D, Section~\ref{sec:finite})}.
\item The conclusion of \textup{(a)}, \hyp{POLE} and \hyp{TD-TRI}, together with the finite facts of Section~\ref{sec:finite}, imply \hyp{H}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
% (a),(b): d2f6b988 (SMALLHOST-D + Cor. H-RED8), 898eb5ab (H-RED6). (c): 71dbf09e (H-RED14 (c)), via 7d38a456 (TRI-BRICK-TD), 87fba731 (3-cut brick), b35d0429 (TD-EXT). All llm+crosscheck-audit passed.
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
(a) Given $g,t$, \hyp{FE-EXIST-D}$_{10}$ provides $(P,C)$ with $[g\in P]=t$, and \hyp{FE-EXT-D}$_{10}$ colours $H^D$ with $P$ as colour class $6$. (c) By induction over cyclic $3$-edge-cuts of the host: a $3$-edge-cut with distinct ends whose one contraction has a dominant pole and whose other contraction is EX1-good yields EX1-goodness of the whole graph (the ``3-cut brick''); \hyp{POLE} supplies dominance. At a minimum triangle side carrying exactly one digon the pole is not available in the needed form, and \hyp{TD} is used instead (Lemma TRI-BRICK-TD). The finite facts cover the hosts with $14$ vertices and the small contractions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The main reduction theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Main reduction; ROOT-CS4]\label{thm:main}
If \hyp{FE-EXT-D}$_{10}$, \hyp{FE-EXIST-D}$_{10}$, \hyp{POLE}, \hyp{TD-TRI} and \hyp{II$_D$} hold, then \hyp{H} and \hyp{II} hold, and every finite loopless multigraph with maximum degree at most $3$ has a star edge colouring with $6$ colours.
\end{theorem}
% 6010cb59 ROOT-CS4 (llm+crosscheck-audit, audit passed; novelty unchecked). Chain: d2f6b988 (2) + 71dbf09e (c) => (H); 52408ddd => (II); 6bfcd4d5 => DMS.
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:hred}(a),(b),(c) give \hyp{H}; Theorem~\ref{thm:rh2}(b) gives \hyp{II} and DMS.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Variants]\label{rem:variants}
The same conclusion holds with \hyp{TD-TRI} replaced by an ``L-shape'' form \hyp{TD-L-TRI} (which implies it), and with \hyp{POLE} replaced by a single boundary-extension statement \hyp{W-EXT} (``one universal W-colouring per M-port'', which implies \hyp{POLE}). \section{Finite cases certified by computation}\label{sec:finite}

\subsection{Method}
Every finite fact below has the form ``every member of an explicit finite family is EX1-good'' (or a pole-dominance or leaf-colouring analogue). Certification in \Lean{} follows one pattern: the family is represented by an explicit list of edge lists (orbit representatives under automorphisms where applicable); for each representative, edge $g$ and $t\in\{0,1\}$, the certificate supplies an explicit colouring; a Boolean checker (e.g.\ a star $6$-colouring checker with a neighbour table, and a check that colour class $6$ is a PM with the prescribed status of $g$) is defined in \Lean; its \emph{soundness} theorem (checker returns \texttt{true} $\Rightarrow$ the mathematical property holds) is proved; and the checker is evaluated on the data by the kernel (\texttt{decide}), not by compiled code. Hence no trust in external code is needed for the certified statements, but the \emph{completeness} of a census list (that every graph of the class is isomorphic to a listed one) is, unless stated otherwise, a separate computation.

\section{Star colourings of special families}\label{sec:classes}

For integers $n\ge 5$ and $1\le k<n/2$ the generalized Petersen graph $GP(n,k)$ has vertices $u_i,v_i$ ($i\in\mathbb{Z}/n$) and edges $u_iu_{i+1}$, $u_iv_i$ (\emph{spokes}), $v_iv_{i+k}$.

\begin{theorem}[Spokes as a colour class]\label{thm:gp2spokes}
For every $k\ge 5$, $GP(k,2)$ has a star edge colouring $c$ with $6$ colours such that $c^{-1}(6)$ is exactly the set of spokes. \textup{(\Lean-proved.)}
\end{theorem}
% 1262231d GPSpokesMC.gp2_tables (llm+lean-gate+crosscheck-audit, passed; novelty "new"). Contract P07-LEAN-GP2.
Thus these graphs satisfy the ``MC'' form (a PM as colour class) for a specific, prescribed perfect matching.

\begin{theorem}[Star $5$-edge-colourable families]\label{thm:five}
Each of the following graphs has a star edge colouring with $5$ colours:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the flower snarks $J_n$, $n\ge 5$ odd;
\item the Goldberg snarks $G_k$, $k\ge 5$ odd;
\item every finite simple graph admitting a covering map to the Petersen graph; every finite simple graph admitting a covering map to $K_4$;
\item $GP(2m,k)$ for $m\ge 3$ and odd $k<m$; $GP(4m,k)$ for $m\ge 2$ and odd $k<2m$;
\item $GP(10m,4)$, $GP(14m,6)$, $GP(17m,8)$, $GP(22m,10)$, $GP(26m,12)$, $GP(30m,14)$ for every $m\ge 1$;
\item every graph obtained from a loopless cubic multigraph by replacing each vertex by a copy of the Petersen graph minus a vertex (more generally, an ``all-good'' inflation).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover star colourings pull back along covering maps: of simple graphs, and of multigraphs along parallel-respecting covers.
\end{theorem}

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, DMS for loopless multigraphs follows from:

\begin{problem}[\hyp{FE-EXT-D}$_{10}$; contract P18, with the case $D=\emptyset$ as P09]\label{prob:p18}
Prove Statement~\ref{stmt:five}\,\hyp{FE-EXT-D}$_{10}$.
\end{problem}
For $D=\emptyset$ the global part is reduced to local data (Theorem~\ref{thm:rl} and the reductions after it). The remaining single-exchange local lemma is a joint constraint problem in a bounded zone around the exchange (``ZONE''). Each piece is easy in isolation, but the joint choice under crowding is not: a deterministic greedy fails on $189$ of $3{,}189$ instances with $n\le 14$, although a small search repairs them; and labelled radius-$2$ neighbourhoods are essentially all distinct, so no finite catalogue will close the lemma. A crowding-tolerant argument is needed. With several exchanges at the minimum allowed distance $3$, the local zones interact in most instances, so the multi-exchange case is not a sum of single-exchange problems.
% obstacles gm 567dd586 (ZONE), gm 383c539a (multi-exchange). Not facts.

\begin{problem}[\hyp{FE-EXIST-D}$_{10}$; contract P16]\label{prob:p16}
Prove Statement~\ref{stmt:five}\,\hyp{FE-EXIST-D}$_{10}$.
\end{problem}
This is a pure existence statement (a PM with prescribed status of $g$ plus an edge set; no colouring). The version over all of $\cD$ is false (Remark after Theorem~\ref{thm:main}), so any proof must use cyclic $4$-edge-connectivity of the host.

\end{problem}
